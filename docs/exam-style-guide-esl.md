# Exam-style guide: Grade 4 ESL (Cambridge Primary ESL Stage 5), End of Semester 1

This guide says HOW the Grade 4 ESL EOS1 exam asks questions. It never says WHAT it asks. Everything you write
must be new: new texts, new talks, new people, new places, new situations. Also read the "Reserved situations"
list in `docs/stops-brief.md`: those situations must never appear anywhere in the trail.

## Totals at a glance
| Paper | Minutes | Parts | Questions | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 Listening | 25 | 3 | 15 | 15 |
| Paper 2 Reading and Use of English | 35 | 6 | 34 | 34 |
| Paper 3 Writing | 35 | 2 tasks | 2 | 20 |
| Total (written papers) | 95 | | | 69 |

Paper 4 Speaking (5 to 10 minutes per student) is not covered by the trail.

## Level and language
- Learners: Vietnamese children aged 9 to 10, Cambridge Primary ESL Stage 5. Level about CEFR A2 (Lexile 650L to 800L).
- Length and difficulty sit BETWEEN Grade 3 and Grade 5: shorter texts and talks than Grade 5, fewer clauses per sentence, more everyday vocabulary, but the same kinds of traps (paraphrase, changed plans, words from the text used in a wrong option).
- British English spelling and vocabulary (colour, favourite, centre, practise (verb), programme, mum, football, holiday, autumn, timetable, film, pounds (£) for prices).
- Short, clear sentences. Every text must be easy to picture. Friendly, school-age situations (school, family, friends, clubs, weekends, food, health, the town, celebrations). No brands, no real celebrities, no real living people.
- Names: a mix of international names (e.g. Theo, Ruby, Kai, Zara, Felix, Anya, Omar, Isla, Ravi, Lena, Hugo, Priya, Sam, Jade, Tom, Kenji) and some Vietnamese names (e.g. Minh, Lan, Huy, Mai, An, Bao, Linh, Khoa). Do not reuse a name inside the same stop for different people. A short checker list of names that must NOT be used is kept privately by the checker; if `scripts/overlap_check.py` flags a name, change it.
- Thinking levels (Webb DOK). Level 1 = find or recognise something said or written directly. Level 2 = understand a paraphrase, infer, compare, decide what someone means, choose the grammar form that fits the context. Level 3 is NOT used in the Listening or Reading papers. The test specification asks for at least 40% Level 1 and at least 20% Level 2 in Listening, Reading and Use of English. In every trail set of 10 test items we use 5 Level 1 + 5 Level 2.
- Text length targets from the test specification (Grade 4): reading texts are short (under 40 words), medium (80 to 100 words) or long (150 to 250 words); a whole Reading paper has 500 to 700 words of text. Listening talks are short (under 20 seconds), medium (20 to 59 seconds) or long (60 to 100 seconds); the Listening paper lasts 20 to 25 minutes with instructions. Use of English stems are short (under 15 words), medium (15 to 50) or long (over 50).

## The four EOS1 units (topics for all texts, talks and tasks)
1. Talking about people: describing personality (talkative, generous, hard-working, outgoing, shy, bad-tempered, selfish, nervous, cheerful, lazy, calm, quiet); making the world a better place (kind acts, helping others); role models and inspirational people, their achievements; favourite people; adjectives with prepositions (good at, interested in, worried about, proud of, afraid of, kind to).
2. Food and health: common illnesses and symptoms (sore throat, runny nose, fever, cough, headache, stomach ache, feel dizzy, sneezing, earache); the food plate and a balanced diet (grains, cereals, dairy products, fats and sugars, protein, vitamins, minerals, fibre, calcium); mosquitoes and staying safe from bites (nets, repellent, standing water); health blogs; a folk tale about sharing food; quantities (a bowl of, a pinch of, a bunch of, a sack of, run out of).
3. Places: where you live and what you like about it; street art and pictures that trick the eye (sketch, shade, pattern, curved); describing places past and present (there was / there were, used to be); imaginary and fictional places (magical, mysterious, enormous, exotic, incredible); a lost city story; adjectives and adverbs that compare.
4. Special occasions: celebrations and holidays (fireworks, costumes, a feast, decorations, lanterns, lights, gifts); personal celebrations (birthdays, weddings, receptions); a traditional food for a festival (boiled, soft, chewy, sweet); solving problems (time zones, working things out); a birthday party story.

What the real paper tests most: the Use of English items (15 of the 34 Reading and Use of English marks, in Parts 1, 2 and 4) are spread almost evenly, about two items each, over 5Ug.04 (past simple), 5Ug.05 (present continuous, including future plans), 5Ug.10 (modals), 5Us.01 (quantifiers), 5Us.02 (indefinite pronouns), 5Us.04 (defining relative clauses) and 5Uv.01 (adjective + preposition), with 5Ug.11 (adjectives and comparatives) once or twice. 5Ug.03 and 5Uv.05 are not tested directly in Reading but are fair game. 5Ug.10 (modal verbs) and 5Ug.11 (comparatives / superlatives) are also named in the Writing task instructions and marked in the writing rubric, so the trail should give these two the most practice.

## Paper 1: Listening (25 minutes, 15 marks, 3 parts, 15 questions, 1 mark each)
Every recording is played TWICE. Each part starts with a short spoken introduction (part number, what you will hear), then a silent pause for reading the questions, then the recording, a short "Now listen again", and the second playing. Instructions on the paper are one or two lines. All answers are written on the question paper (no separate answer sheet).

Timings on the real recording: the whole recording is about 11 minutes (Part 1 about 4 minutes 45 seconds, Parts 2 and 3 about 3 minutes each); the rest of the 25 minutes is for reading, answering and checking. The voices are British adult actors (a woman and a man, also playing children and parents).

- **Part 1 (5 questions, 5 marks): pictures A B C.** The question is printed AND read aloud before each talk ("One. Where ...?"). Three black-and-white line pictures, A B C, under each question. Each talk is a short dialogue between two speakers (a child and a parent, or two friends of different sexes), 4 to 6 turns, 15 to 20 seconds (short talk), played twice in a row with "Now listen again" and a pause of about 5 seconds between plays and before the next question. Question types (for example): how someone travels, why someone feels a certain way, which kind of thing someone likes, where or when people will meet, which object someone buys or chooses, a time, a price, the weather, an activity. All three pictures are mentioned or clearly suggested in the talk; only one answers the question. Every real item has a trap: an option ruled out for a reason (broken, closed, sold out, the wrong size), a wrong guess corrected by the other speaker ("No, that wasn't it..."), a first plan replaced by a new one ("We were going to..., but now..."), a thing someone did not like versus what they choose instead. The answer usually comes in the second half of the talk. DOK: the real part is mostly Level 2 (elimination, corrections, changed plans); in the trail make about 2 Level 1 (said directly) and 3 Level 2.
- **Part 2 (5 questions, 5 marks): notes completion.** One talk by ONE speaker (a teacher talking to her class), about 75 to 80 seconds and about 140 words (long talk), giving practical information about a trip or a week away. A spoken introduction says who is speaking and about what, then 10 seconds to read the form. The printed form has a title in capitals, a small picture, one example line already completed, and 5 labelled lines with gaps (label on the left, gap on the right). Instruction: "Write one phrase or a number or a date or a time." Some gaps have printed words around them, so the child writes only the missing part: a day of the month before a printed month, one activity before a printed "or ...", the second number of an age range, the second item after a printed "... and", a price after a printed £ sign. Answers are one word or a number (dates in any sensible form). There is NO spelled name at Grade 4. Traps: a time said right beside the date, a different activity at a different time of day, something that happened last year but will not happen this year, a weekday to bring money that is not the date asked for, and extra information about what the price includes. DOK: Level 1 (all 5, information said directly, but the child must pick it out from a nearby distractor).
- **Part 3 (5 questions, 5 marks): conversation, three written options.** One conversation between two friends (a girl and a boy), about 70 seconds and about 150 words (long talk), about something one of them did and a plan. The printed rubric names the two speakers and the topic; 20 seconds to read the questions. Five questions in the order of the talk, each with three short written options A B C (mostly 1 to 4 word noun phrases; one question is a sentence stem "X didn't want to ... because" with three full-clause endings). Question types (for example): who someone went with, when or where something happened, what the weather or a place was like, what someone liked best or did NOT like (negative questions are used), why someone felt or decided something, what they will do next. All three options are mentioned or suggested in the talk: the other speaker guesses two wrong options before the real answer is given, something expected did not happen, comparisons decide the answer (one thing better than another, a third thing the best), a reason that belongs to the OTHER speaker, a first plan replaced by a better one, and an event that already happened last week. DOK: about 1 to 2 Level 1, 3 to 4 Level 2.

Question format mix: 10 of 15 are multiple choice (67%) and 5 are written answers (33%), inside the specification's 60 to 80% multiple choice and 20 to 40% close questions.

## Paper 2: Reading and Use of English (35 minutes, 34 marks, 6 parts, 34 questions, 1 mark each)
The paper's own name on the cover is "ESL – Reading"; the answer key calls it "Reading and Use of English". Every part begins with a worked example (except Parts 5 and 6). Total text on the paper is about 650 to 700 words.

- **Part 1 (5 questions, 5 marks; Use of English).** Five separate sentences about ONE child and ONE event (a short connected story told in order), plus an example sentence. Each sentence has one gap (________) and three words printed underneath; students circle one. Stems are 10 to 25 words. The three options are always the same kind of word, so only grammar decides: three forms of one verb (base / past / -ing), three prepositions after an adjective, three quantifiers, three relative words (who / which / where type), three indefinite pronouns (some- / any- / no- / every- type), or three forms of one word family (participle adjective in -ed, adjective in -ing, noun). Traps: a time clue elsewhere in the sentence, countable vs uncountable noun after the gap, person vs thing before the relative, the meaning of the whole sentence after "but" or "because". DOK: about 2 Level 1, 3 Level 2.
- **Part 2 (7 questions, 7 marks; Reading and Use of English mixed).** Seven short real-life texts, each drawn inside a picture frame: a sticky note, a phone text message, a school notice, a sign, an email. 20 to 40 words each, each text signed or titled. Two kinds of question:
  - Comprehension (4 of the 7): "Why has X written to Y?", "What does the notice mean?", "What does X want Y to know?", "What should people who ... do?". Three options A B C that PARAPHRASE the text; the right one does not repeat the text's words exactly. Wrong options reuse words from the text but change the meaning: all vs some, before vs after a day, a change of place vs a change of day or of what to bring, a rule for one group of people applied to another group, ask vs tell, must vs can.
  - Grammar gap (3 of the 7): one word or phrase in the text is replaced by a gap and the question is "Which option correctly completes the message / notice?". Three options A B C of the same kind: three verb forms for a plan (base, past, present continuous), three modals for a rule on a notice, three modals at the start of a polite question. The worked example at the top of the part is also a grammar gap (a preposition).
  - DOK: grammar gaps and direct comprehension Level 1; "why / what does it mean" items Level 2. The real part is roughly 3 Level 1, 4 Level 2.
- **Part 3 (5 questions, 5 marks).** A conversation between two friends of about 12 turns. One friend's 6 lines are printed; the other friend's first line is the worked example (letter already filled in) and the next 5 lines are gaps. A list of 8 sentences A to H is printed beside the conversation: one is the example, five are answers, two are extra. Students write a letter in each gap. The conversation is about a plan (where, how long, how to get there, what to see). To choose, students must check the line BEFORE (a question needs an answer; a "How ...?" question needs a way or a method) and the line AFTER (a sympathetic reply follows bad news; "Exactly!" follows a guess or suggestion; "Good." follows a solution; a "so" summary follows a fact). The two extra options are on topic and use words from the conversation but do not fit both sides of any gap. DOK: Level 2.
- **Part 4 (7 questions, 7 marks; Use of English).** An informal letter or email from one child to a friend (about 140 to 150 words, with a title, greeting and signature), with an example gap and 7 numbered gaps. A WORD BOX of 10 words is printed above the letter: the example word, the 7 answers and 2 extra words. Instruction: write ONE word in each space; each word can be used only once. Gaps test grammar words, one per objective where possible: the word after a comparative, a past form of "be" (there was / there were), a relative pronoun, the preposition after an adjective, the auxiliary of a present continuous future plan, an indefinite pronoun, a quantifier. The two extra words in the box are short grammar words (for example prepositions) that look possible but do not fit any gap. Because of the word box, this part is a choice from a list, not free recall. DOK: about 3 Level 1, 4 Level 2.
- **Part 5 (5 questions, 5 marks).** One magazine-style page made of THREE short first-person texts by three children (about 90 words each), each on the same theme but about a different person or experience. Five questions on the facing page: "Which person ...?" / "Who ...?" with options A B C = the three children's names. A name can be the answer more than once (or not at all). Questions PARAPHRASE one detail or idea in one text (a reason a plan changed, an effect on the writer's own future, a belief someone holds, what a small action shows, a quality the writer admires or lacks). Distractors: the other two texts mention something similar (all three may talk about helping, effort or a hobby) but only one says exactly what the question asks. DOK: Level 2 (all 5).
- **Part 6 (5 questions, 5 marks).** One longer text (about 190 to 210 words, 5 short paragraphs) with a title and a one-line introduction, a third-person magazine piece about one child's experience. Five questions A B C in the order of the text, roughly one per paragraph: what started the experience, the child's feeling or opinion about one part of it (often a change from what they expected), why the child did or did not do something, what an adult thought of the child's work, and the child's plans or wishes at the end. Each option is a full sentence. Wrong options are built from real details in the text combined wrongly, or give the idea of OTHER people in the text (friends, classmates) instead of the child's own. There is NO "writer's purpose" question at Grade 4. DOK: about 2 Level 1, 3 Level 2.

## Paper 3: Writing (35 minutes, 20 marks, 2 tasks, 10 marks each)
Both tasks have the same shape. Each has a title line ("Task 1: ..."), one instruction sentence, "Include all of the following:" and 4 or 5 bullet points, then a language reminder (use connectives; use modal verbs / use comparatives and adjectives) and "Check your spelling, grammar and punctuation." Lined space follows. There are no pictures, no letter or email layout and no title to invent (the title is given).

- **Task 1 (10 marks): explaining and advising, for readers in general.** The prompt asks for 1 to 2 paragraphs (7 to 8 sentences) that explain and give advice on a topic from the units. No word count is printed; 7 to 8 sentences at this level is about 60 to 90 words. Four content bullets ask the writer to say what people should do, what good habits to follow, what to avoid, and why it matters. A fifth bullet names the target grammar: use modal verbs (should, shouldn't, must, mustn't, need to) and the unit's vocabulary. The topic comes from one of the four units.
- **Task 2 (10 marks): describing a personal choice.** The prompt asks for 1 to 2 paragraphs (7 to 8 sentences) about a choice the writer makes, real or imaginary. Four bullets: what it is and what it looks like; what makes it special or interesting; what you would do with it or there; why you chose it. Language reminder: use descriptive adjectives, at least one comparative or superlative, and connectives.
- **Marking (from the real rubric in the answer key, the same five criteria for both tasks, 0 / 1 / 2 points each, 10 per task):**
  | Criterion | Max | 2 points means |
  |---|---|---|
  | Task Achievement and Content (5Wc.02, 5Wc.03) | 2 | every bullet point covered and developed with relevant detail |
  | Organisation and Layout (5Wor.02, 5Wca.03) | 2 | clear paragraph(s), logical order, suitable connectives |
  | Grammar Range and Accuracy (5Wca.04 plus 5Ug.10 in Task 1, 5Ug.11 in Task 2) | 2 | a range of mostly accurate sentences INCLUDING the named target grammar (modal verbs in Task 1; at least one correct comparative or superlative in Task 2); errors minor |
  | Vocabulary and Spelling (5Wca.02) | 2 | varied topic vocabulary (Task 1) or descriptive adjectives (Task 2); familiar words spelled correctly |
  | Handwriting and Fluency (5Wca.01) | 2 | clear, legible handwriting; writing flows |
  1 point = some of it (a bullet missing or thin, weak links, frequent errors, target grammar wrong); 0 = off topic, no organisation, errors block meaning. These categories and maxima are taken directly from the real Grade 4 rubric; no Grade 5 scaling was needed. In the trail, Handwriting cannot be marked on screen: treat it as "Fluency" (sentences read smoothly, full sentences, correct capital letters and full stops) or leave it for the teacher, and say so in the feedback.
- Test specification: Writing has 2 tasks; total student writing for the paper is 70 to 100 words in the spec table, while each real task asks for 7 to 8 sentences. Trail models should be 7 to 8 sentences and about 60 to 90 words each.
- DOK (spec, Writing): at least 10% Level 1, 20% Level 2, about 50% Level 3 (extended writing).

## Item-writing rules (Grade 4)
1. Three options in every multiple-choice question (A B C). One is clearly right. The other two must be tempting but clearly wrong for a careful reader or listener. Never four options.
2. Options about the same length. Do not make the right answer the longest or the only full sentence.
3. Keep Grade 4 sentences short: most under 15 words, none over 25. One idea per sentence in listening scripts. Use the unit vocabulary listed above.
4. Every wrong option gets a short, kind "why" (one sentence) that tells the child what to check.
5. Write the "worked" line as a teacher would explain the answer in one or two short sentences, quoting the key words of the text or talk.
6. Hints point at the strategy, never give the answer ("Listen to what she says AFTER 'but'.", "Is the noun after the gap countable?").
7. Spread the right answer position (A, B, C) evenly.
8. No trick questions, no answer that depends on world knowledge outside the text or talk.
9. Grammar items test ONE point each, with the clue inside the same sentence (a time word, a countable noun, a person before "who").
10. In listening, every picture or option must be mentioned or clearly suggested in the talk; the answer is never the first thing mentioned in more than about one third of items.
11. Written answers (Listening Part 2, Reading Part 4) must have one clearly correct form. Accept sensible spelling variants of dates (3rd, 3, third) and numbers written as digits.
12. Never copy sentences, names, situations or writing prompts from any real exam paper or published book. Check new content against the Reserved situations list.
