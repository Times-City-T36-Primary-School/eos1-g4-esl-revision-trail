# The nine stops: what each one covers (Grade 4, Cambridge Primary ESL Stage 5)

All stops use the four Semester 1 unit topics as the setting, spread evenly: 1 Talking about people; 2 Food and health; 3 Places; 4 Special occasions (sub-topics and vocabulary in `exam-style-guide-esl.md`).
Level: children aged 9 to 10, about CEFR A2. Shorter and simpler than Grade 5. Follow the text and talk lengths in the style guide.

## Per-stop notes
- Stops 1 to 3: "Learn it" = one-step questions on the rule (mcq, tfill with one word). "Exam style" = items in the format of Reading Part 1 (one sentence of 10 to 25 words with a gap "________" and 3 words of the SAME kind as options; mcq) and Part 2 grammar gaps (a 20 to 40 word note or notice as the stim with one gap; "Which option correctly completes the message?"; mcq), and Part 4 (one gap of a short letter, tfill with a word box in the stim). Lightning = quick grammar or word choices.
- Stop 4: every listening item has an audio stim. Learn it: Level 1 numbers, times, prices (£), days and dates (pmcq with tags); Level 2 traps: changed plans, a wrong guess corrected, an option ruled out (broken, closed, sold out, too big) (pmcq); Level 3 notes: short talks (20 to 40 s) with 1 or 2 gaps each (tfill), the gap answer said close to a distractor (a time beside a date, last year vs this year). Exam style: Level 1 and 2 = Part 1 (pmcq, two speakers, 4 to 6 turns, 15 to 20 s); Level 3 = Part 2 (one teacher's talk of about 140 words / 75 s with a notes form; 3 rounds = 3 gaps of the SAME talk; the second variant uses a second talk). NO spelled names at Grade 4. Test: items 1 to 5 (DOK 1) = the 5 gaps of ONE Part 2 talk (v[1] of each uses a second talk with its own 5 gaps; the line shows the label and any printed words around the gap, e.g. "Date: {0} July", "Price: £{0}"); items 6 to 10 (DOK 2) = 5 Part 1 pmcq.
- Stop 5: every item has an audio stim. Learn it: short exchanges (20 to 40 s) with one mcq each: who, when, where, what someone liked best or did NOT like, why, what they will do next. Exam style: Part 3 format; a level = 3 questions on ONE conversation between a girl and a boy (about 150 words / 70 s); the other speaker guesses wrong options, a plan changes, a comparison decides. Test: 2 conversations (items 1 to 5 on the first, 6 to 10 on the second; v[1] uses two more conversations), DOK 1 = said directly, DOK 2 = paraphrase, reason, opinion.
- Stop 6: Learn it: Level 1 notice words (must, mustn't, need to, don't have to, before, until, only, all, some); Level 2 short messages (why has X written? what does X want?); Level 3 conversation logic (what follows a question, bad news, a suggestion, a solution). Exam style: Part 2 (a short text in the stim, 20 to 40 words, signed or titled; 3 options; some items are grammar gaps in the text) and Part 3 (bank items with a dialogue stim of about 12 turns and an 8-option bank A to H; one used in the example line "0"). Test: items 1 to 5 = Part 2 (DOK 1: grammar gaps and direct meaning), items 6 to 10 = Part 3 from ONE dialogue (DOK 2).
- Stop 7: Exam style Part 1 = a 5-sentence mini story about ONE child and ONE event, one mcq per sentence (the sentence in q with "________"; three words of the same kind). Part 4 = a letter or email stim (about 140 words, title, greeting, signature) with 7 numbered gaps and a WORD BOX printed at the top of the stim html ("Word box: after, which, ..." with 10 words: the example word, the 7 answers and 2 extra words); each item is one gap (tfill; the line shows the sentence with the gap; ans = the box word). Test: items 1 to 5 = Part 4 gaps from ONE letter (DOK 1 and the easier ones), items 6 to 10 = Part 1 sentences of ONE story (DOK 2). Label DOK honestly; if you need, reorder so that the five DOK 1 items come first.
- Stop 8: Exam style Part 5 = a stim with THREE short first-person texts by three children (about 90 words each, same theme, different person or experience), and mcq items "Which person ...?" whose 3 options are the three children's names (opts[0] the right name). Answers may repeat a name. Questions paraphrase; the other two texts mention something similar. Part 6 = a third-person magazine text about one child's experience (about 190 to 210 words, 5 short paragraphs, title + one-line introduction) with mcq questions in text order (what started it, a feeling that changed, why the child did something, what an adult thought, plans at the end). NO "writer's purpose" question. Test: items 1 to 5 = Part 6 (some DOK 1), items 6 to 10 = Part 5 (DOK 2).
- Stop 9 (Writing): see "Writing stop" below.

## Writing stop (Stop 9) special format
"practise": [GAME_LEARN, WRITE_DESK]
GAME_LEARN ("s9r", "Learn it"): mcq / tfill items on writing skills: choose the right modal for advice or rules (should, shouldn't, must, mustn't, need to); choose a comparative or superlative (bigger than, the most exciting); choose a linking word (first, also, because, so, but, finally, for example); choose a good topic sentence or ending; which bullet point is missing from a short answer; correct a spelling or grammar mistake; is this answer long enough (7 to 8 sentences)?

(The puppy task above is only a format example: do NOT use it.)
Write 3 Task 1 tasks (explain and advise, 4 or 5 bullets, the last bullet or the prompt asks for modal verbs) and 3 Task 2 tasks (describe a personal choice, real or imaginary: what it is and looks like, what is special, what you would do, why you chose it; use adjectives and at least one comparative or superlative). min 60, max 90, needTitle false (the title is given). Each task has 3 models: Developing (a little short, 50 to 58 words, 2 or 3 typical learner errors), Secure and Excellent (inside 60 to 90 words). Each model has "marks" with EXACTLY these keys and values 0 to 2: content, organisation, grammar, vocabulary, fluency. The rubric (the school's real Grade 4 categories, 2 marks each, 10 per task):
- content = Task achievement and content (all bullets covered, relevant ideas)
- organisation = Organisation and layout (clear order, one or two paragraphs, linking words)
- grammar = Grammar range and accuracy (modals in Task 1, comparatives and superlatives in Task 2, correct verb forms)
- vocabulary = Vocabulary and spelling (topic words, high-frequency words spelled correctly)
- fluency = Fluency (complete sentences with full stops and capitals, easy to read; on paper this is "Handwriting and fluency")
Notes: one per category, in that order, each starting with the category name and the mark, e.g. "Organisation and layout 2/2: ...". Totals: Developing about 4 to 5/10, Secure about 7 to 8/10, Excellent 10/10.
RESERVED for Stop 9 (and the Summit Writing paper): Task 1 must NOT be about how to stay healthy (balanced diet, healthy habits). Task 2 must NOT be about a place you would like to visit.
Test (10 items): mcq / tfill on the same writing skills (5 DOK 1, then 5 DOK 2).
